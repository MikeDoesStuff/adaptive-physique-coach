# Strength, Nutrition, and Recovery

## Lifting and Physique

Start with the intended active programme and recent completed sets. A dated programme inside an export is not automatically the current plan. Respect exercise preferences, equipment access, time limits, discomfort and the user's chosen priorities.

Compare reps/load at similar RIR, range of motion, technique, equipment, rest and set position. Machine changes, assistance weight, unilateral loading and dumbbell conventions can change interpretation. Preserve exact exercise/equipment identities until equivalence is established. Do not call less machine assistance a load reduction or add body mass to every exercise without a defined convention.

Exclude warm-ups from working-set totals. Report straight working sets separately from drops, myo activation/mini-sets, and other techniques; they do not automatically represent equal independent hypertrophy sets. The supplied CSV records `(L)` and `(R)` set variants. Track per-side exposure and do not silently double each muscle's paired set count. If estimating muscle-group exposure, distinguish direct and indirect work and explain the counting convention. App aggregate sheets are cross-checks, not extra sets.

Progress an established rep-range prescription using reps and then load when technique and target RIR are maintained. Select the smallest useful adjustment instead of rebuilding the programme each week. A deload or stable strength during a cut can be appropriate. Use multiple comparable exposures to investigate a decline.

Assess physique changes with a combination of weight trend, consistently taken waist/girth measurements when available, performance and optional standardized photos. Verify measurement units and technique. Do not infer precise body-fat percentage, muscle gained/lost, a date for visible abs, or spot reduction from the available exports.

## MacroFactor Nutrition

The user's latest documented MacroFactor goal and targets govern nutrition planning. Read the latest relevant settings update, goal status, weekday targets, weight trend, actual weigh-ins, recorded calories/macros, and partial-logging flags. Old context targets remain historical until corroborated or confirmed. A faster-loss trial described as optional does not become active by mention.

MacroFactor estimates expenditure using intake and weight history. It intentionally does not use wearable exercise energy to set nutrition targets. Evaluate logging coverage and actual observations before interpreting its estimate; do not add tracker cardio calories to its target or subtract them to create a second deficit. [MacroFactor expenditure](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/20-expenditure), [MacroFactor on wearable calories](https://macrofactor.com/wearables/).

Distinguish prescribed targets from actual intake. Absence of a partial flag alone does not establish complete logging. Low isolated intake rows, missing days and long runs of unchanged model values need context. A freshly exported weight trend may continue beyond the last actual weigh-in. Do not describe its last value as a current measured weight or assume daily generated values supply sufficient recent evidence.

When data supports it, compare seven-day weight averages with the preceding seven days, and app trend changes across comparable dates. State the dates, weigh-in count, kg change, and denominator for percentage change. A 14-day percentage change is a 14-day change; divide appropriately to describe an average weekly rate. With sparse observations, report the limitation instead of manufacturing rolling means through zero fill or unexplained interpolation.

Discuss alignment with the active goal alongside strength, hunger, energy, recovery and adherence. Propose an app-setting review when supported. Do not mechanically alter calories every time cardio changes or prescribe an aggressive deficit from old incomplete logs. New intake/macro recommendations need current goals, reliable coverage, and relevant dietary context. Preserve the user's preferences and allow MacroFactor's coached adjustments to remain the operating system.

## Recovery and Adaptation

Use recent sleep, RHR, HRV, steps, subjective state, symptoms and performance together. Compare provider measurements with the user's same-source baseline. One readiness score or noisy HR reading does not decide a session. Historical sleep averages and statements of no pain are not current clearance.

If data is old, continue programme or historical analysis but keep today's readiness recommendation conditional on current fatigue, pain/illness and availability. When recent evidence shows poor recovery, consider reducing optional/harder cardio, lowering the local leg demand or shortening the session. Preserve effective lifting where tolerable and investigate nutrition/recovery as needed. Do not assume cardio is the cause of every performance decline.

For a proposed change, state what observation would support keeping it and what would prompt review: for example, comfortable breathing during the new modality, no worsening symptoms, manageable next-day leg fatigue, and preserved performance at the next comparable lower-body session. Avoid a universal HRV percentage or workload ratio as an injury prediction.

Prepare a dated coaching record:
