# General Cardio

## Purpose and Intensity

Choose cardio to improve aerobic fitness, support activity and enjoyment, and fit the user's lifting and recovery priorities. A run can be replaced by incline walking or stairs for a general aerobic goal. Report the replacement accurately; it does not supply the same running-specific practice or prove maintained 5K/10K ability.

Treat modality as open-ended. The decision model is purpose + elapsed/active duration + documented effort + movement demands + individual recovery, not a fixed exercise menu. Hiking, dance, sports, classes, intervals, circuits, adaptive activities and mixed sessions can all contribute. For an unfamiliar activity, retain its original label, ask about relevant demands if needed, and analyse available duration/effort without inventing pace, machine settings or specialized scores.

Separate aerobic work from skill practice, pauses and resistance components when the record supports that distinction. A class or sport's whole elapsed time is not automatically continuous vigorous cardio. For mixed intervals, preserve work/rest and segment intensity where available; a mean HR or whole-session RPE does not establish every minute's category. A circuit containing lifting is not automatically additional hypertrophy volume.

Use documented breathing/talk-test effort and the user's stated RPE scale, supported by reliable HR trends. Device zone names and percentages are not interchangeable physiological definitions. Do not automatically import running HR targets to another modality or call an uncalibrated session precise `Zone 2` work.

The CDC describes moderate relative effort as about 5-6/10 and vigorous as 7-8/10, with speech becoming restricted at vigorous effort. The user's historical running notes used 3-4/10 for easy running. Preserve the stated scale and effort cue rather than relabelling it. Full sentences alone may describe light or moderate activity; do not assign a health-guideline category without enough evidence. [CDC intensity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html).

For weekly health context, compare documented moderate and vigorous minutes with the adult guideline of 150 moderate minutes, 75 vigorous minutes, or a combination, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days. Count moderate-equivalent minutes as moderate + 2 x vigorous only when classification is supported. Unknown-intensity minutes remain separate. This is a population health benchmark, not a demand to increase immediately or an equivalence in fatigue across modalities. [CDC adult activity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html).

## What to Compare

| Modality | Useful progression evidence | Important context |
|---|---|---|
| Incline treadmill walking | Duration, speed with units, grade in percent, breathing effort, HR trend at comparable settings | Machine/model, rail support, incline changes, prior lifting, calves/Achilles/feet, HR signal quality |
| StairMaster or stepmill | Duration, machine/model, level or steps/min, breathing and leg effort, next-session response | Levels are machine-specific; preserve measured floors/steps without inventing vertical distance; rail support and quadriceps/glute/calf fatigue |
| Outdoor/treadmill running | Duration, pace/distance when trustworthy, effort, comparable HR, route/conditions | Terrain, temperature, running exposure, impact tolerance, treadmill calibration |
| Bike, elliptical, rower, other cardio | Duration and effort, plus within-machine power/cadence/resistance where meaningful | Technique, equipment differences, local fatigue, preference and joint tolerance |
| Sports, dance, classes, hiking, circuits, unfamiliar or mixed activity | Actual active duration, documented effort, work/rest structure and repeatable task measures | Pauses, skill demands, load carried, impact, resistance components, conditions and individual tolerance |

Incline percent is not degrees or a resistance level. A stored machine level is not equivalent to another machine's level. Wearable distance is not necessarily belt distance. Note rail support because it affects comparability; advise a manageable setting and comfortable posture rather than removing support needed for balance.

Track breathing effort and local muscle fatigue independently. Comfortable breathing can coexist with substantial leg fatigue on stairs. Pain is separate from exertion and should influence selection immediately. Avoid describing stairs as a recovery session solely because they have little running impact.

## Prescribing or Replacing a Session

1. Identify the session purpose: easy aerobic volume, general moderate cardio, enjoyable activity, an optional harder stimulus, or running-specific practice.
2. Select a preferred modality that is tolerable today. Start from recent documented tolerance. When changing modality, use a conservative trial rather than translating a long run minute-for-minute by default.
3. Specify duration and an effort cue. Use known machine settings as a starting reference, not a required incline/level or a calorie-burn target. Adjust settings to maintain the intended effort.
4. Consider its placement beside lifting. When lifting is the priority, substantial cardio usually follows lifting or sits in a separate time slot. Brief easy warm-ups are different. Reduce or move leg-demanding cardio if it degrades lower-body performance.
5. Give a shorter fallback and a progression criterion. Hold effort/settings steady and extend duration modestly after repeat tolerable sessions, or adjust one setting while holding duration. No mandatory weekly percentage increase, long session, intervals, or maximal testing.
6. Reassess symptoms, adherence, sleep, leg fatigue, and the next comparable lifting exposure. Retain a well-tolerated plan; a weak workout alone does not establish cardio interference.

Example format, with values selected from current evidence:

```text
Purpose: easy aerobic session
Modality: preferred incline treadmill walk
Duration: based on recent completed tolerance
Effort: comfortable sustained conversation, no leg burn target
Settings: start at recent tolerable speed/grade; adjust to effort
Fallback: shorter session or gentler modality when legs are tired
Progression: change one variable after repeat sessions with good recovery
```

Concurrent aerobic and strength training can be compatible with muscle growth and maximal strength. An updated meta-analysis found no average impairment in those outcomes, with a greater concern for explosive strength, especially in the same session. This supports individualized monitoring rather than declaring all cardio harmful or promising stairs have no recovery cost. Evidence does not establish a universal treadmill-versus-StairMaster ranking. [Schumann et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8891239/).

## Missing Machine Detail

A completed `Stair Climbing (Duration Only)` entry with 900 seconds establishes 15 minutes of stair cardio; no level, HR, incline, RPE or precise physiological zone is available from that row. Its parent gym duration is not additional stair time. A `Walking` entry establishes walking, not an incline treadmill setting. These examples illustrate interpretation, not required sources or activities.

Use such records for documented session/minute totals. Ask for machine settings or effort only when needed for progression, comparison or substitution. A manual note can fill gaps without replacing device originals. The optional [Cardio Log](../assets/cardio-session-log.csv) supplies additional fields; it is not a requirement to duplicate every export.

## Reporting

Report aerobic minutes by modality, known intensity and source coverage, with overlap resolved or labelled. Compare within modality and similar settings. Session duration x session RPE may be a useful descriptive workload measure when both are actually recorded on a consistent scale; label its units arbitrary and do not convert it to TSS or an injury-risk prediction. RIR from lifting is not cardio RPE.

Describe useful progress as more tolerable duration, greater within-machine output at similar effort, lower comparable effort, improved adherence, or stable lifting alongside cardio. Calories burned, distance and maximum HR alone do not establish progress.
